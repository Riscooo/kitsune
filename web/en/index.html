Privacy

Strict or Comfortable: you choose.

You change it from your main user's card and it applies to all of Kitsune. Before applying it, Kitsune tells you in two lines what it means.

Strict

Clears cookies, sessions and cache on close. Strict tracker blocking, anti-fingerprinting and HTTPS only.

Comfortable

Keeps sessions and cookies on close. Standard blocking that breaks fewer sites. No telemetry.

Your home

A home page that is yours.

  • Light or dark. The switch crosses the screen with a streak of ink. Try it with the button at the top.
  • Your background. Pick a built-in one or upload your own. It also shows on every new tab.
  • Your shortcuts at hand, the clock and a search box without distractions.
  • The window your way: buttons on the left or the right, and the logo if you like.

Lots of tabs

A lot open, never lost.

Spaces

Work, school, fun: each space with its own tabs, its color and, if you like, its own user. Switch with a click or with Ctrl Alt 1…9.

Tabs on the side

On top, in a column on one side or just the icons. Pick it in Appearance, no restart.

Split view

Up to four sites at once: two with their divider, three in columns or four in a grid. Ctrl Alt D splits or adds another.

Glance

Alt and click a link: it opens on top, without a new tab. Esc closes it; if you want to keep it, turn it into a tab.

Palette

Ctrl K and type: tabs from every space, bookmarks, history and every action with its shortcut.

Compact mode

Just the site. The toolbars come back when the mouse reaches the edge. Ctrl Alt C turns it on and off.

Inks

Six inks, and yours.

An ink sets the colors of all of Kitsune, in Light and in Dark, and its shape: square, soft or round corners, and sans or serif lettering. Change it with one click in Appearance. Every ink reads at least as well as Paper: we check it before accepting it.

Saver mode

Light when you need it.

One button in the home settings and Kitsune uses less memory and less battery. No restart, and it turns off just as fast.

60 fps
Animations capped at 60 frames per second.
Fewer processes
Open sites share processes and take up less memory.
15 min
Tabs left unused for 15 minutes go to sleep.
1, 2 or 4 GB
You set the memory limit. Above it, the oldest tabs go to sleep.

Twenty sites open, four minutes.

The memory each browser uses with the same 20 sites (news, video, social, shops, documentation), adding up all of its processes. Without saver mode, Kitsune uses as much as Firefox; with it, half as much, and less than Chrome.

Questions

How much does it cost?

Nothing. Kitsune is free and has no ads.

What is it based on?

On Gecko, the open-source engine Firefox uses too. We build it ourselves, with our name, our privacy settings and without Mozilla's services (accounts, telemetry, studies). Gecko's code is distributed under the MPL 2.0 license.

What data does Kitsune send?

Kitsune sends no usage data, crash reports or studies. It does connect to keep your extensions and uBlock Origin's lists up to date.

Can I bring my bookmarks and passwords?

Yes. The first time you open Kitsune it offers to bring them over, and later whenever you like from the palette (Ctrl K, «Bring from another browser»). From Zen, Firefox, LibreWolf, Floorp or Waterfox it brings bookmarks, history and open tabs (from Zen, its spaces too); from Chrome, Edge or Brave, with the import wizard. Passwords from Zen or Firefox move over with their CSV export.

Windows warns me when installing it. Is it safe?

The installer is not digitally signed yet (it costs money and will come later), so Windows SmartScreen says it does not recognize the app. If you downloaded it from this site, click «More info» and then «Run anyway». Kitsune's code is public on GitHub.

macOS won't let me open it. What do I do?

It is not signed with an Apple certificate yet. The first time, go to System Settings → Privacy & Security and click «Open Anyway». If macOS says it is damaged, open Terminal and type xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Kitsune.app.

How do I install it on Linux?

Extract the archive (tar -xf Kitsune-*-Linux-x86_64.tar.xz) and run ./kitsune/instalar.sh. It installs for your user, without sudo, in ~/.local/share/kitsune, and shows up in your applications menu. To update, do the same with the new version; to remove it, ~/.local/share/kitsune/desinstalar.sh.

Can I have several accounts at once?

Yes, like in Google: click your avatar on the home page and pick another user. That tab becomes theirs and the rest stay as they were. Each user has their own sessions and cookies; history, bookmarks and passwords are shared.

Why does Strict mode sign me out?

Because it clears cookies when Kitsune closes: that way no site recognizes you between visits. If you would rather stay signed in, switch Kitsune to Comfortable (on your main user's card).
